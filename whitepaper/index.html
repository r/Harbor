Bring your own AI to the web.

Take your agent with you as you browse. You decide what it can see, what it can do, and what it shares with each site.

Try Harbor in Firefox → Read the proposal →

Harbor is an experimental browser extension and a proposal for the web. Built by people inside Mozilla as an independent project.

Whose side is your agent on?

Suppose you ask an airline’s assistant to find you the cheapest way home. Would it put a train ticket first?

The airline has every reason to build an assistant that sells flights. You have every reason to bring one that remembers you’re happy to take the train. Why should visiting a website settle which of those assistants gets to help you?

You can already follow a link in the browser of your choice. We think you should be able to bring your own agent along. A site can say what it offers. You should get to decide who acts on your behalf.

What if you could use your own AI on your local paper’s site?

Your grandmother sold tickets at Union Station. You’ve told your agent about her. You open your local paper’s archive.

You choose “Search with my AI” and ask, “What did people say when the old station closed?” Your browser asks, “Let the paper use your AI to answer this question?”

You allow it. Your agent picks three words, Union Station closure, and sends them to the archive. The paper sends back matching stories. Your agent points you to the ticket clerks’ recollections because it knows about your grandmother. Her name never appeared in the search.

Now suppose your agent wants to search for her by name. Does that permission cover sending her name, or would you want it to show you the search and ask again before sending her name to the newspaper?

A.

Your model.

Today you connect Harbor to a model on your computer or through a provider. Choosing a model doesn’t import another assistant’s conversations or memories. Your agent could learn about your grandmother from this conversation or from a saved note you give it permission to read.

B.

Your context.

With a model running on your computer, the prompt is processed on your device. With a hosted model, the provider receives the prompt, including any conversation or saved-note text sent with it. If that text mentions your grandmother, the provider sees that detail. In this example, the newspaper receives just the search terms.

C.

Mediated by the browser.

When a site asks to use your agent, you decide what to allow. Your browser enforces your choice. As with camera access, you can give one site permission and take it back later.

What can a website do with Harbor?

A page can prompt the user's model, run an agent against tools the user has approved, or register a tool of its own.

1 · use the user's modelwindow.ai
// The page never sees an API key. The browser does the routing.
const session = await window.ai.createTextSession();
const reply   = await session.prompt(`Summarize this article: ${document.querySelector("article").innerText}`);
2 · run an agent that can call tools the user has approvedwindow.agent
for await (const ev of window.agent.run({ task: "find recent press on quantum chips" })) {
  if (ev.type === "tool_call") console.log("→", ev.tool);
  if (ev.type === "final")     console.log(ev.output);
}
3 · a page declares its own tool · Harbor APInavigator.modelContext
navigator.modelContext.addTool({
  name:        "search_archive",
  description: "Search our 20-year archive",
  inputSchema: { type: "object", properties: { query: { type: "string" } } },
  handler:     ({ query }) => searchArchive(query),
});

A site usually picks the model behind its assistant. Harbor lets each visitor bring their own.

What could you build with Harbor?

01

A docs site that reads itself for you.

The docs site sends the article text to the reader’s chosen model through window.ai. The site never needs the reader’s API key.

await window.ai.createTextSession().then(s => s.prompt(article))

02

PDX to SFO, Friday after five, aisle seat.

Your agent can search the airline’s flights and ask it to hold a seat.

window.agent.run({ task: "PDX → SFO Friday after 5pm, aisle" })

03

Open a chapter by name.

The agent calls the reading site’s open_chapter tool.

addTool({ name: "open_chapter", inputSchema: { … } })

04

Revise a draft in your own voice.

When you ask for help revising a draft, a writing extension could send the text and your style profile to your chosen model.

window.ai.createTextSession({ systemPrompt: userStyleProfile })

A smaller surface, by design.

We assume a model can be fooled by something it reads. In the proposal, a site asks for specific actions. You could allow an agent to search a news archive without giving it permission to edit a story. You can revoke a site’s access.
